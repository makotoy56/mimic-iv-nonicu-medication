# Prediction-time evaluation protocol: confirmed ward revision

This additional analysis is separate from the existing RAAS association analyses and their retrospective-discrimination classifier. It does not replace their cohorts, estimates, adjustment sets or published AUCs. The initial no-prior-ICU protocol was fixed before its model inputs/performance were viewed. During cohort review, recorded ICU absence alone was recognized as insufficient to confirm a target ward. This documented amendment adds observed ward-location eligibility before the revised ward-confirmed models are run. Initial working aggregates are retained privately; no feature/estimator tuning was performed using the holdout.

## Target and information times

- Two prediction times: hospital admission (0 hours) and admission + 24 hours, using the source DATETIME clock. The outcome is death after that prediction time and no later than recorded hospital discharge. This is a variable hospital-stay horizon, not 7-day/30-day mortality or post-discharge survival.
- Start with the existing adult hospital-admission base, including observation/day-case admissions. At each prediction time require confirmed survival and continued hospitalization strictly beyond that instant and internally consistent admission/discharge/death records. Missing or inconsistent eligibility/outcome timing is excluded with explicit reason counts. Exact-boundary deaths/discharges are excluded.
- At **both** prediction times require a single confirmed non-ICU inpatient ward interval in native `transfers`: `intime <= prediction_time < outtime`, with known interval end and unit. Duplicate records of the same unit are allowed; overlapping different units, unknown/missing unit/end, and no covering interval are unconfirmed and excluded with counts. No unit entered after the prediction time is used to fill an earlier location. Future exit times later than the point are interchangeable; neither the later destination nor a future ICU transfer selects the cohort. Admission-time results apply only to this ward-confirmed subset; they do not establish performance for admissions whose initial ward is unavailable or which are still in the ED.
- The explicit ward allowlist includes general, specialty, intermediate/stepdown and hospital observation units; it excludes ICUs, ED/ED observation, PACU, discharge lounge, nursery and unknown units. The mapping is fixed from unit designations, not outcomes. Bed/event timestamps reconstruct historical location but do not prove when the source record was entered or revised.
- Require no recorded ICU entry at or before that prediction time, using the earliest evidence from both `icustays` and ICU-labelled `transfers`. Unknown ICU entry timing cannot verify eligibility and is excluded. **Later ICU transfers remain included**. Selecting on no ICU use over the entire admission would use future information; that rule remains unchanged for the original association cohort only.
- Primary baseline models use reconstructed admission age and recorded sex only. These demographics are assumed known at admission; source field-entry/version history is not available to prove operational availability. Untimestamped admission descriptors, insurance, race, calendar group, future lengths of stay, identifiers, discharge fields and future ICU status are not predictors. The deliberately limited predictor set is not a comprehensive severity model.
- A prespecified 24-hour companion model adds a verified RAAS prescription-record indicator. It requires prescribed start from 0 through <24 hours **and** a matching POE order made by the 24-hour prediction time. POE matches use subject/admission/order IDs; duplicate keys are reduced to first recorded order time and audited. Unmatched/unknown or later order times do not make the feature positive and are counted. Zero means no confirmed qualifying record available by that time, not proof of no prescription or administration. Eligibility never depends on this feature's later/missing records. Final order status, later discontinuation links and stop times are not used. Snapshot contents may have been revised; original field-version reconstruction and actual administration are not established.

## Frozen estimation and evaluation

1. Three models: admission age/sex; 24-hour age/sex; 24-hour age/sex plus time-verified RAAS record.
2. Unweighted sklearn L2 logistic regression, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=2000. Median age imputation/scaling and categorical imputation/encoding are fitted in training data only. No class balancing, test-set tuning, feature selection or post-hoc recalibration.
3. One patient-level 80/20 split, seed 42, created on the sorted adult base before the time-specific eligibility filters. The same patient allocation applies to both times; every admission of a patient stays in one partition. This is internal random-patient validation, not a temporal/external/prospective validation.
4. Cross-check the actual held-out ROC/AP with independent Mann-Whitney/threshold-group rank formulas (tolerance 1e-12). Report eligible/train/test admissions, patients, deaths, overlap and test prevalence; ROC AUC, average precision (the existing PR-AUC naming convention), Brier score, mean predicted risk and observed risk. Diagnose calibration intercept/slope and quantile-bin calibration without changing predictions. Constant train-prevalence Brier is a reference.
5. For ROC AUC, average precision and Brier, use 500 prespecified held-out-patient bootstrap draws carrying all eligible admissions, seed 20261118 + prediction hours. The two 24-hour models share draws. These percentile intervals condition on the fitted training model and do not include model-training uncertainty. Count invalid one-class draws without replacing them; require at least 475/500 successful draws. Calibration-bin event-rate intervals use these same patient draws with fixed score bins; no claim of exact coverage.
6. Only aggregate tables, receipts and figures are exported. No identifiers, individual probabilities, split membership, fitted per-patient records or raw model inputs are written to public outputs. Inputs remain in memory in the authorized local environment. Query job metadata and source hashes provide provenance.

## Data access and limits

Use the existing BigQuery project/dataset and read-only SELECTs. Run a dry run and enforce a 10 GiB per-query billed-byte cap. Keep existing sandbox expiration/billing settings. Do not alter the five construction SQL files or original exposure definition. The new SELECT joins the adult base to native transfers, icustays, prescriptions and poe. Check admission grain, keys, timestamp types, duplicate POE links, missing order-time coverage and exclusion counts; cross-check eligible counts with a separate SQL predicate before modeling.

References: [MIMIC prescriptions](https://mimic.mit.edu/docs/iv/modules/hosp/prescriptions.html) defines prescribed start/stop times and POE linkage; [MIMIC POE](https://mimic.mit.edu/docs/iv/modules/hosp/poe.html) defines ordertime as when the provider order was made. Prescription records do not establish administration.

Differences between admission and 24-hour metrics have different populations and risk sets and must not be described as model improvement. The 24-hour companion is a prediction-feature comparison, not a treatment-effect estimate. Any results retain the limited-feature, retrospective-data, single-internal-split, approximate-age, timestamp/version-history and endpoint-ascertainment limitations above. Prespecification prevents test-outcome-driven choices; it is not external registration or clinical validation.

Ward interval source: [MIMIC transfers](https://mimic.mit.edu/docs/iv/modules/hosp/transfers.html). A deterministic sample of actual transfer histories is compared locally with the Python interval reconstruction; only match counts and job metadata are exported. Location uncertainty is reported separately from verified non-target locations.
