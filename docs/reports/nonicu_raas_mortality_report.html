Documented Early Inpatient RAAS Prescription Exposure and In-Hospital Mortality in Adult Non-ICU Admissions

A MIMIC-IV Clinical Data Analytics and Real-World Evidence Portfolio Report

Author

Makoto Yoshida, PhD

Published

November 18, 2026

1 Analytical Report Scope

This report presents the analytic outputs from an admission-level observational study of documented inpatient renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) prescription exposure within the first 24 hours and in-hospital mortality among adult non-ICU admissions in MIMIC-IV v3.1.

The emphasis is on reproducible clinical analytics: cohort construction, exposure definition, covariate handling, outcome modeling, absolute risk interpretation, and Python numerical checks and patient-aware evaluation.

The results remain hypothesis-generating. The design estimates associations after adjustment for measured covariates; it does not establish treatment effectiveness.

The original unpenalized primary/sensitivity fits have separation and remain nonconverged audit outputs. Supplementary Firth fits all converged; corrected landmark ordinary/proxy ORs were 0.356 (95% patient-cluster CI 0.292–0.423) and 0.382 (0.315–0.453). The separate unweighted L2 risk model returned AME -0.3797 percentage points (95% patient-cluster CI -0.4201 to -0.3407). Patient-disjoint discrimination and patient-cluster intervals are added validations; cohort/exposure definitions and full-data point estimates are preserved. All estimates remain observational.

2 Reproducible Workflow

The workflow separates data construction, model estimation, visualization, and validation. The analytic sequence starts with an adult hospital admission cohort, excludes ICU-associated admissions, classifies documented ACE inhibitor or ARB prescription exposure from records with start times within 24 hours, fits adjusted outcome models, and checks aggregate outputs and numerical diagnostics in Python.

The key design choice is that the fixed non-ICU cohort is constructed before exposure definition or outcome modeling. The SQL eligibility rule does not use the prescription-exposure flag, and downstream outputs are traceable to one admission-level table. Excluding ICU use over the entire admission can still condition on post-admission events; this procedural ordering does not establish statistical independence or remove selection bias.

3 Cohort And Exposure

The analytic cohort contained 460,786 adult non-ICU hospital admissions. Documented inpatient RAAS prescription exposure within 24 hours was observed in 56,825 admissions, or 12.33% of the cohort. The cohort includes observation/day-case categories and is not a strict inpatient-only filter. The unit of analysis was the hospital admission (hadm_id); the cohort was not restricted to one admission per patient, so patients could contribute multiple admissions.

7 Unpenalized Model Diagnostics

The original unpenalized primary and four sensitivity models have separation and did not converge. Nominal intervals and p-values from these fits are not validated inference. Current finite OR summaries use the explicitly supplementary Firth fits, with patient-cluster intervals reported separately.

Notebook 04b_multivariable_outcomes.ipynb regenerates the primary parameter diagnostics and five-model convergence comparison. Historical nonconverged OR figures and the old coefficient display are excluded from the current report.

All original adjustment categories, including sparse admission types, are retained. The documented separation is a numerical/inferential limitation; the original model specification has not been changed to obtain convergence.

9 Patient-Disjoint Retrospective Discrimination

The patient-disjoint holdout is an internal evaluation of retrospective discrimination on the full admission cohort. It uses prescription records from 0–<24 hours while including deaths within 24 hours, and applies no 24-hour survival/continued-hospitalization eligibility filter. There is no fixed clinical prediction time or prospective prediction horizon: ROC AUC 0.860635 and PR AUC 0.117607 do not describe admission-time or 24-hour prognostic performance. Patient separation and train-only preprocessing address patient overlap and preprocessing leakage, not temporal feature availability. The 24-hour landmark association analyses separately require confirmed survival and continued hospitalization strictly beyond 24 hours and evaluate subsequent in-hospital death; they use a different eligible cohort.

A patient-disjoint 80/20 patient holdout (seed 42) has zero patient overlap. The same class-balanced L2 classifier specification is used; its scaler and categorical encoder are learned on training data only. The admission-random reference is a new deterministic-order calculation, distinct from the historical unordered-row AUC. A single split does not provide external validation, and the different case mixes preclude a claim that group splitting improved performance.

10 Absolute Risk Interpretation

The adjusted odds ratio is a relative measure. Because in-hospital mortality was uncommon in this non-ICU cohort, the project also reported adjusted predicted risks and average marginal effects. The fitted model produced an average adjusted predicted mortality difference of approximately -0.38 percentage points between the documented-exposure and no-exposure scenarios.

Source notebook: 04b_multivariable_outcomes.ipynb.

Because this is an observational model-based estimate, the curves should be interpreted as adjusted predicted probabilities under the fitted model, not as causal treatment effects. The age curves hold other covariates at a typical mean profile. No exposure-by-age interaction was fitted; variation in absolute contrasts is a model implication, not a formal interaction test.

12 Interpretation Boundary

The finite supplementary Firth estimates and the separate L2 risk contrasts were directionally consistent. The original unpenalized MLE remains nonconverged; numerical agreement alone would not establish valid inference. The analysis remains observational.

The main sources of potential bias are residual confounding by indication, unmeasured comorbidity burden, acute physiologic severity, outpatient medication history, prescribing behavior, medication discontinuation, dose, duration, adherence, and selection effects related to early survival and treatment eligibility.

Exposure was based on inpatient prescription records. It did not directly measure outpatient chronic RAAS inhibitor use, medication adherence before admission, confirmed inpatient administration, dose, treatment indication, or duration of therapy. The outcome was limited to in-hospital mortality.

The unit of analysis is the hospital admission (hadm_id). Historical profile intervals assumed independent admissions. Added patient-cluster bootstrap intervals and patient-disjoint evaluation address repeated admissions separately. These changes do not remove residual confounding or the selection induced by excluding any ICU stay over the entire admission.

14 Conclusion

Documented early inpatient RAAS prescription exposure was associated with lower in-hospital mortality in this adult non-ICU MIMIC-IV cohort after adjustment for measured covariates. The association was also presented on the absolute risk scale, where the difference in adjusted predicted mortality risk was small in percentage-point terms and varied by age.

The portfolio value of the project is the reproducible EHR clinical analytics workflow: transparent cohort and prescription-exposure definitions, multivariable modeling, absolute risk interpretation, cautious observational framing, a visible 24-hour landmark sensitivity analysis, and documented Python numerical checks using current aggregate outputs.
